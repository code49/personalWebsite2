I first took **18-100: Introduction to Electrical and Computer Engineering** during my first semester at Carnegie Mellon. For me and most incoming students, this class was the gateway to the department. 18-100 does more than teach circuit fundamentals and code; it inspires students to pursue ECE while setting the foundation for collaborative problem-solving.

The core philosophy of 18-100 centers on close, personal mentorship between TAs and students. Learning happens through small-group lab sessions, accessible office hours, and a dedicated team of TAs who support students beyond circuit analysis or programming.

Because 18-100 recruits mostly freshman and sophomore TAs, it creates a near-peer mentorship model: first-year students gain accessible role models, while young TAs discover a passion for teaching while reinforcing fundamental engineering concepts.

### teaching assistant

My desire to give back led me to join the course staff as a **Teaching Assistant (TA)** from August 2023 to January 2025:
- **Small-Group Labs & Recitations:** Led weekly lab sections to translate theory into practical steps.
- **Office Hours & Debugging:** Guided students to debug their own circuit problems, encouraging patience and persistence.
- **Student Mentorship:** Helped students navigate academic transitions and adapt to CMU's rigorous environment.
- **Grading & Feedback:** Graded assignments, lab reports, and exams with detailed feedback.

### head teaching assistant

Building hands-on pedagogical experience prepared me to serve as **Head TA** from January to December 2025, managing course logistics and mentoring the teaching staff:
- **TA Mentoring:** Guided TAs on effective teaching practices, active listening, and supporting students through academic adversity.
- **Course Direction & Mascot:** Collaborated with faculty to keep course content modern, engaging, and supportive. The staff even established a custom course mascot, **Watt**son!
