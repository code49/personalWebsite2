I first took **18-100: Introduction to Electrical and Computer Engineering** during my very first semester at Carnegie Mellon. For me - as for the vast majority of incoming students - this class was the gateway. 18-100 holds a unique, critical opportunity: it doesn't just teach the basics of circuits and code; it inspires students to pursue ECE studies. It sets the table for the study skills, collaborative practices, and problem-solving mindsets they will carry through the rest of college and their lives. 

Because of this, the core philosophy of 18-100 centers on close, personal mentorship between TAs and students. Rather than relying on distant lecturing, the course structure facilitates learning through small-group lab sessions, highly available office hours, and a dedicated team of TAs who support students beyond the technical scope of basic circuit analysis or programming. TAs and students frequently collaborate late into the night and over weekends—and genuinely love doing so.

Furthermore, as an introductory class, 18-100 recruits mostly freshman and sophomore TAs. This creates a double-sided mentorship model: it gives first-year students accessible, near-peer role models, while providing the young TAs a space to discover a genuine passion for teaching and helping others, all while reinforcing their own understanding of fundamental engineering concepts.

### teaching assistant

My own desire to give back to this community led me to join the course staff as a **Teaching Assistant (TA)**, serving from August 2023 to January 2025. In this role, I focused on building supportive learning environments by:
- **Leading small group lab sections and recitations** to break down complex theory into intuitive, practical steps.
- **Conducting office hours** where I focused on guiding students to solve their own circuit debugging problems, encouraging patience and persistence.
- **Mentoring students** through academic transitions, helping them adapt to CMU's rigorous environment.
- **Grading assignments, lab reports, and exams** with detailed feedback, treating grading as an extension of teaching rather than just evaluation.

### head teaching assistant

Building up this deep, hands-on pedagogical experience—on top of having first walked in the students' shoes—prepared me to transition into the **Head TA** role, which I held from January to December 2025. As Head TA, I had the privilege of helping set the direction of the class, managing overarching course logistics, and mentoring the next generation of educators:
- **Mentoring other TAs** on effective teaching practices, active listening, and how to best support students through academic adversity.
- **Setting the class direction** alongside the faculty to keep the course content modern, engaging, and supportive of student mental health. The teaching staff even established a custom course mascot, **Watt**son!
